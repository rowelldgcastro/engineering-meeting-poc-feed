# Knowledge transfer: why the gearbox uses a 7.5:1 helical reduction

**Priya:** Thanks for making time. I'm taking over the conveyor drive and I keep getting asked why we didn't use a worm gear. Can you walk me through it?

**Tom:** Sure. Back in 2021 we compared a worm gear at 30:1 against a two-stage helical. We ended up with the helical at 7.5:1 plus the belt stage, giving the same overall ratio.

**Priya:** What drove that?

**Tom:** Efficiency, mostly. The worm we quoted was about 70 percent efficient at that ratio. The helical is around 96 percent. With the motor running 16 hours a day, the losses on the worm would have needed a bigger motor, a 5.5 kW instead of the 4 kW we have now.

**Tom:** We also had heat. The worm box would have run hot in the enclosure, and we measured 78 degrees C on a similar unit in the old line, which is over the 70 degree limit for the grease we use.

**Priya:** But the worm is self-locking, right? Wouldn't that help on the incline section?

**Tom:** People think that, but we didn't want to rely on back-driving resistance for safety. We put a spring-applied brake on the motor instead. That was a firm rule from the safety review: holding the load has to be a dedicated brake, not the gearbox.

**Priya:** Makes sense. Anything you'd do differently?

**Tom:** I'd specify the belt stage tensioner better. We had belt slip in the first year because the tensioner was undersized. We fixed it with an automatic spring tensioner. If you redesign, put the automatic tensioner in from the start.

**Priya:** Good to know. Is there documentation on the efficiency comparison?

**Tom:** There's a memo, I think it's called DR-0457, in the old design review folder. I'm not sure it has the heat measurement though, that was in my notebook.
